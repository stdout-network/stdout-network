# Code of Conduct

We started as a confederation of people who mostly know each other from an industry with sharp elbows. Leave them at the door, especially as people from outside ad tech join.

The [Charter](CHARTER.md)'s "How we show up" is the positive version of this page. This page is for when that isn't enough.

This code follows the spirit of the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), which we recommend reading. Where they differ, this document and the [Social Contract](SOCIAL_CONTRACT.md) apply.

## What we expect

- Assume good faith, and argue with ideas rather than people.
- Make room: newcomers, people outside ad tech, and people who are quieter in meetings get heard.
- Credit others' work, including in talks, posts and pitches about the project.
- Keep commercial interests visible. Say when your employer has a stake in a discussion.
- Respect the co-chair system: two co-chairs share a role equally, and nobody is "the real one".

## What we don't accept

- Harassment, discrimination, or demeaning language, in any project space or at project events.
- Sharing anyone's personal information without consent.
- Using the project, its members or its data to advantage your employer in ways members would not agree to, such as harvesting buyer contacts for your day job.
- Sustained disruption of discussions or decisions.

## Peace and Consensus Committee

Reports go to the Peace and Consensus Committee, 3 to 5 members elected by the Assembly for 6 months. No committee member sits on the Confederal Council at the same time, and anyone involved in a report steps aside from it.

**How to report:** email the committee's private address (set at the Founding Assembly) or message any committee member. You can report anonymously; it may limit what the committee can do.

**What happens:**

1. **Listen.** A committee member acknowledges the report within 3 days and asks what outcome you are hoping for.
2. **Repair first.** Where it is safe and the people affected agree, the committee mediates: a conversation, an apology, an agreed change in behaviour.
3. **Act when needed.** If repair fails or is not appropriate, the committee can issue a warning, suspend someone from project spaces, or recommend removal of membership.
4. **Removal** needs the committee's unanimous recommendation and can be reviewed by the Assembly on appeal.
5. **Publish outcomes, not names.** The committee publishes an anonymised summary of cases each quarter.

Urgent safety concerns go straight to any committee member or Council co-chair, who can suspend someone immediately while the committee looks into it.
