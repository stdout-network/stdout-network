stdout Network

Ads for the moments developers wait.

A member-run ad network for the terminal. Developers opt in, keep most of what their screen earns, and nothing about their code leaves their machine. The network belongs to the people who build it: one member, one vote.

The audience nobody else reaches

Developers spend their day in the terminal, where browser ad blockers don't reach and no ad network has ever been invited. stdout shows one short line at five terminal moments when someone is waiting or has just won, plus one in the status bar of their coding agent, and only to people who opted in.

  • Targeted by project, never by person. A buyer can ask for "UK developers whose project uses Postgres". The match happens on the developer's own machine.
  • Opt in, always. Nothing runs unless you typed adterm. No install scripts, in line with npm's rules since 2019.
  • Never in the chat. Inside Claude Code and other agents, ads never go into a tool result, the conversation or the model's context. That would be prompt injection with a CPM. Ads are also off in CI and non-interactive shells.
  • The status line is for people. Coding agents have a status bar that only the human sees. That's the one place stdout runs while an agent works: the developer watches the agent, and the model never reads the line.
  • Never reads the conversation. In the status line, adterm reads one thing from the agent: the project's folder, to match ads to its stack. It never opens the transcript, and never looks at the model, the cost or what you asked the agent to do.
  • Say what you want to see. adterm --interests add postgres tells it what you'd like ads about. Interests stay on your machine and are matched there, like everything else.
  • Text only, sanitised. No escape sequences, no clipboard writes, no "curl | sh" copy.

Privacy by design

The ad request carries two things: the moment and the tool name. No IDs, paths, package names or project names. The seller sends back a short slate of eligible ads with their targeting rules, and adterm decides locally which one fits.

Built for agents

Advertisers' AI agents buy stdout inventory over AdCP 3.2, the open Ad Context Protocol: discover products, create a media buy, upload creatives, read delivery. Each moment is its own product, so a buyer can target the agent status line (tty_agent_statusline) on its own, or alongside terminal moments, with the same country and stack targeting. Every request and response is validated against the official AdCP 3.2.3 schemas.

Who runs it

One rough idea for how it could be run, written up in ideas/. Nothing is decided.

The thought: start with a group of people from ad tech, then open to developers and anyone else who fancies a slightly whacky experiment in confederalism. Power sits in small, self-governing communes and rises through delegates who can be recalled.

  • One member, one vote. Employers, sponsors and advertisers get a voice, never a vote.
  • Co-chairs, not bosses. Every commune has two, on staggered six-month terms.
  • Guarded against capture. No more than a third of Council delegates may share an employer.
  • Decided in the open. Every decision goes in a public log; if it is not in the log, it was not decided.
