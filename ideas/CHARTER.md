# The stdout Charter

Four minutes to read. Sign it by adding your name to [MEMBERS.md](MEMBERS.md), and you're in.

## Why we're doing this

Software is getting cheap. Any of us could vibe a CRM over a weekend, which is why we keep doing it. Features, dashboards, integrations, whole products: if you can describe it, a machine can build it.

What a machine can't build is the stuff that was always doing the real work. The person who picks up when you call. The intro that lands because of who it came from. Knowing who to trust in a room. Years of favours, owed and repaid.

So this is a bet on that. stdout Network is an ad network for developer terminals, and we'll build it properly. But it's also an excuse to make something together, in the open, and to look after each other while the ground moves. It starts with about sixty of us from ad tech, and it won't stay that way.

The code is the toy. The people are the point.

## What we believe

1. **Relationships are the moat.** Code is cheap to copy. Trust isn't. We invest in each other first and the product second.
2. **Fun is a requirement.** If it stops being fun, that's the bug we fix first.
3. **Ship, then talk.** A demo beats a deck. A pull request beats a meeting. Nobody needs permission to make something.
4. **Taste beats volume.** Machines can make infinite stuff. Our job is choosing what's worth making, and making it well.
5. **Nobody gets left behind.** Ad tech lays people off in waves. When it happens to one of us, the rest close ranks: leads, intros, references, a pint, and the fighting fund.
6. **Fair by default.** The people who make it work get paid first: developers, then good causes, then us. We never sell anyone's data, because we'd hate it done to us.
7. **Power is shared and passed on.** No titles worth having, no permanent bosses. The best idea wins, including when it comes from the newest person here.
8. **Bring someone with you.** A community that only looks like its founders slowly dies. Each of us brings in someone the industry usually overlooks.

## How we show up

- **Show and tell, monthly.** Anyone who built something, however daft, gets five minutes and a round of applause.
- **The pub, quarterly.** In person. The beer pot pays when there's money in it; we pay when there isn't.
- **Intros within 48 hours.** When a member asks for an introduction, answer within two days, even if the answer is "I don't know them, but try X".
- **A buddy for every new member** for their first month.
- **Credit loudly, critique kindly.** Name who did the work, every time. Disagree with ideas in public and with people in private.
- **Leave your employer at the door.** We all work somewhere. Here, nobody has a quota: no selling to each other, no vendor pitches in the chat. Job leads for a member who needs one are the exception, and always welcome.

## What we won't do

- Sell data. Anyone's.
- Run an ad we'd be embarrassed to see in our own terminal.
- Let one person or one company own it.
- Turn it into a startup that needs an exit. If you want to commercialise it, fork it: the licence lets you, and we'll still buy you a pint.
- Let the process get heavier than the work.

## Who it's for

- **Ad tech first.** The founding members are about sixty people from the industry, because the first job is building an ad network and we already know how ads get bought.
- **Then developers.** The ads run in their terminals, so they should get a say in what runs there, and a vote.
- **Then anyone.** Designers, organisers, charity people, the curious: anyone who fancies a slightly unhinged experiment in running things together. Ad tech experience is not required. Being decent is.

The Assembly decides when the door opens. We'd rather it was sooner than later.

## Structure, lightly

For the developers: think of it as Unix. Small tools that each do one thing well, joined by pipes. Communes are the tools, the Council is the pipe, and nobody runs the whole machine.

Until there's money or a falling-out, this charter is all the governance we need. The [Social Contract](SOCIAL_CONTRACT.md), with its communes, co-chairs and votes, exists to protect this charter. It switches on at the [Founding Assembly](FOUNDING_ASSEMBLY.md), which happens before the first pound from an advertiser. If the rules and this charter ever disagree, the charter wins and the rules get fixed.

## Signing

Signing means three things: you'll show up, you'll look after the others, and you'll help keep it fun. It commits your employer to nothing, and you can leave whenever you like, no hard feelings.

To begin with, you join because a member brings you in. Once the door opens, you can also join by turning up: contribute something, and get welcomed by a commune at its meeting. Either way, add yourself to [MEMBERS.md](MEMBERS.md) in a pull request, and the person who brought you, or a co-chair of the commune that welcomed you, approves it. Changing this charter takes a vote of all members at an Assembly.
